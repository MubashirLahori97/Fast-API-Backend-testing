# Lesson 6: The Hypothalamus's Role in Immune System Function

The hypothalamus is the central organ of immune regulation. (*Molecular Psychiatry*, 2005) [Established]

## The hypothalamic rapid immune response [Established]

1. Innate immune cells stimulated by endotoxins, infection or injury release cytokines: IL-1, IL-6 and TNF-α.
2. Sensory nerves and circulating signals carry these cytokine messages to the paraventricular nucleus (PVN).
3. The hypothalamus responds rapidly by releasing CRH and vasopressin.
4. Compared with the pituitary and adrenals, the hypothalamus responds to cytokines quickly, through prostaglandins and nitric oxide.
5. The response also engages the growth hormone, gonadal and thyroid axes.

Communication between the immune and neuroendocrine systems runs both ways, carried by cytokines, neuropeptides, hormones and their receptors. [Established]

## Immune system overview [Established]

- **Innate immunity:** macrophages, dendritic cells, mast cells, natural killer cells, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, complement
- **Adaptive immunity:** B cells and antibodies; CD4+ and CD8+ T cells
- **Bridging both:** γδ T cells and natural killer T cells

## Immune disorders related to hypothalamic dysfunction

- Systemic autoimmune disorders: rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, myasthenia gravis, lupus (SLE), Sjögren's [Emerging]
- Inflammatory bowel diseases [Emerging]
- Adrenal and thyroid autoimmunity: Addison's, Hashimoto's, Graves' [Established for autoimmune basis; Emerging for hypothalamic role]
- Immune-mediated hypertension [Emerging]
- Central diabetes insipidus [Established]
- Autoimmune hypophysitis and hypothalamitis, with antibodies causing hypogonadism, central DI, GH deficiency or hypopituitarism [Established; rare]

## How the hypothalamus regulates the immune system [Established]

The hypothalamus controls the autonomic nervous system, and the ANS regulates both innate and adaptive immunity through its sympathetic and parasympathetic branches.

Hypothalamic and pituitary hormones involved in immune regulation:

- Thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH)
- Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH)
- Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
- Prolactin
- Growth hormone

### Thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) [Emerging]

**Central roles**

- Indirectly stimulates thyroid hormone production
- Influences release of prolactin, growth hormone, vasopressin and insulin
- Influences release of noradrenaline and adrenaline

**Peripheral receptors** are found in pancreatic beta cells, the GI tract, genitourinary system, retina, placenta, thymus, lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow, and immune cells (NK cells, lymphocytes, leukocytes).

**Immune effects** (largely from animal studies)

- Enhances proliferation of spleen and thymus lymphocytes
- Counters corticosteroid-induced suppression of thymus and spleen cells
- Inhibits monocyte activity
- Modulates natural killer cell activity
- Enhances lymphocyte cytokine production

### Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) [Established for HPA role; Emerging for direct immune roles]

- Drives ACTH production, and immune cytokines activate the HPA axis in turn.
- CRH receptors are also found in the adrenals, gonads, GI tract, skin, skeletal muscle, retina, cardiovascular system, thymus and spleen.

**CRH's roles in immunity**

- **Mast cells:** regulates their activity and triggers degranulation, causing histamine reactions.
- **Mononuclear cells:** stimulates lymphocyte proliferation and IL-1 and IL-2 production. IL-1 in turn stimulates hypothalamic CRH release.
- **Natural killer cells:** modulates their activity after stress.
- **Neutrophils:** inhibits IL-1β secretion.
- **Macrophages:** activates TNF and can compromise the endothelial barrier.

### Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) [Emerging]

- **GnRH-I** is the hypothalamic form. **GnRH-II**, expressed outside the brain, helps prevent thymus atrophy. **GnRH-III** stimulates steroid production.
- GnRH is found in immune cells (T cells, mast cells) and the spleen.

**Proliferative effects**

- Increases CD8+ T cells, neutrophils and lymphocytes
- May promote autoimmunity in lupus and type 1 diabetes (animal data)
- Stimulates healing of spinal cord injury

**Suppressive effects**

- Briefly suppresses immunity during embryo implantation
- Inhibits ovarian, endometrial and breast cancer cell lines
- GnRH analogs suppress endometriosis, fibroids and adenomyosis [Established]

### Sleep, prolactin, growth hormone and immunity

**Sleep** [Established]

- Sleep supports immune defense against infection and inflammation.
- Sleep deprivation alters innate and adaptive immunity, creating chronic inflammation and raising the risk of infections and of cardiometabolic, cancer, autoimmune and neurodegenerative diseases.
- Deep sleep inhibits the HPA axis.

**Hypothalamic-pituitary hormones stimulate the thymus** [Emerging]

- **Prolactin** guides maturation of CD4−CD8− thymocytes into CD4+CD8+ T cells, through IL-2 receptor expression.
- **Growth hormone** enhances thymulin release, mediated by IGF-1.

### Thymic hormones [Established]

- The thymus is essential for maturing bone marrow-derived cells into T cells.
- Hypothalamic-pituitary GH and prolactin stimulate release of thymulin.
- Thymopoietin and thymic humoral factor help thymocytes differentiate.
- Thymulin induces T-cell differentiation and enhances T-cell and NK cell activity. Thymulin requires zinc.
- Thymosin, in turn, promotes hypothalamic GnRH and pituitary prolactin and GH release.

### Prolactin's effects on immune cells [Emerging]

| Cell type | Effects of prolactin |
| --- | --- |
| Thymocytes | Promotes differentiation into CD4+CD8+ cells; maintains thymocyte survival |
| B cells | Influences maturation; promotes survival of self-reactive clones; rescues immature B cells from apoptosis |
| Dendritic cells | Increases cytokine production and responsiveness |
| T cells | Modulates early activation; increases TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-2; triggers IL-2-driven proliferation; impairs regulatory T cells; enhances adhesion to blood vessels |
| NK cells | Induces prolactin-activated killer cells in a dose-dependent way; increases IFN-γ release |
| Monocytes | Increases TNF expression |

### Growth hormone's effects on the immune system [Emerging]

- Stimulates T- and B-cell proliferation and antibody production
- Enhances maturation of myeloid progenitor cells
- Modulates cytokine responses
- The thymus, spleen and peripheral blood cells produce GH, and lymphocytes carry GH receptors.
- GH deficiency is not associated with immune deficiency. [Established]

### Excess prolactin [Emerging]

- Elevated or mistimed (dyscircadian) prolactin interferes with immune regulation, impairing elimination of self-reactive B cells.
- Hyperprolactinemia is associated with the development and activity of several autoimmune disorders: lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, antiphospholipid syndrome, systemic sclerosis, celiac disease, thyroiditis, and peripartum cardiomyopathy.
- Dopamine inhibits prolactin, and dopamine agonists have shown clinical benefit in some autoimmune patients.
- Studies report an inverse relationship between prolactin levels and COVID-19 severity, suggesting prolactin may help limit cytokine storm.

## Hypersensitivity

**Immediate hypersensitivity** [Established] — exaggerated or inappropriate immune responses to an antigen or allergen. Type I, II and III reactions occur within 24 hours of exposure.

**Allergies and cortisol** [Established] — cortisol is an important regulator of allergic disease:

- Lung function varies with plasma cortisol levels.
- Circulating inflammatory cell numbers vary with cortisol.
- Low endogenous cortisol may be associated with asthma risk.

**Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS)** [Established as a diagnosis; diagnostic criteria still debated] — abnormal release of variable sets of mast cell mediators, with accumulation of dysfunctional mast cells.

### Mast cells [Established]

- Mast cells are classic neuro-immune-endocrine cells, regulating two-way communication between the nervous and immune systems.
- They sit along blood vessels and body surfaces and carry many receptors, so their development, migration and activation respond readily to the environment.
- They are sentinels of the body, central to defense against the outside world and to stress responses such as allergy.
- Mast cell histamine raises stomach acid and causes vasodilation, itching and bronchospasm.

### Diagnosing MCAS [Established]

- Tryptase is the most specific marker.
- Diagnostic rise: an increase of 20% plus 2 ng/mL above baseline, measured ideally within 1 to 2 hours of a symptomatic event.
- Normal serum tryptase: below about 11.4 ng/mL.

## Hypothalamic stress intolerance and exhaustion [Emerging]

Japanese researchers have proposed a "hypothalamic stress intolerant and exhaustive syndrome," combining autonomic, mood and cognitive, pain and sensory hypersensitivity, and immune hypersensitivity symptoms. Conditions grouped under it include cerebrospinal fluid leak, ME/CFS, fibromyalgia and multiple chemical sensitivity. This is a proposed concept, not an established diagnosis.

## Autoimmune diseases [Emerging]

Autoimmune diseases are characterized by loss of immune tolerance and arise from genetic, environmental, hormonal and immunological factors.

Hypothalamic stress-response systems that contribute:

- HPA axis
- HPG axis
- HPT axis
- Prolactin and growth hormone
- Autonomic nervous system

Neuroendocrine-immune imbalance appears before symptoms in some autoimmune diseases:

- Premenopausal women who developed rheumatoid arthritis before age 50 had low DHEA, cortisol and testosterone, with higher IL-1, TNF-α and IL-6, up to 12 years before diagnosis.
- Hyperprolactinemia has preceded the onset of Graves' disease, dermatomyositis, Sjögren's and lupus.

## Infections

- Lung co-infection (viral plus bacterial) increases activated microglia and inflammatory cytokines in the hypothalamus. [Emerging, animal data]
- The hypothalamus expresses ACE2, the receptor SARS-CoV-2 uses to enter cells, and may be a target of the virus. [Emerging]
- Chronic bacterial and parasitic infections cause endocrine abnormalities, through inflammation of neuroendocrine tissue and through pathogens exploiting the host's hormonal environment. [Established]
- During chronic infection, energy is redirected to the immune system, altering steroid hormone axes. HPA changes then contribute to poor infection control and immune-mediated damage. [Emerging]

### Resident viruses [Emerging; Clinical observation]

- Reactivation of latent viruses (Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus, herpes viruses, HIV) may create hypothalamic micro-inflammation and neuro-immune-endocrine disturbances.
- Stress is a known trigger of EBV reactivation. [Established]
- In Deborah's experience, hypothalamic dysfunction can impair immune competence and allow resident viruses to reactivate, and hypothalamic reactions to COVID-19 can reactivate other latent viruses.
- Hypothalamic dysfunction is a leading hypothesis for many features of long COVID, such as fatigue, sleep, temperature and autonomic disturbances, but it has not been established as the cause.

## Labs

- CBC
- Prolactin
- Autoantibodies as indicated
- Microbial load: EBV (VCA IgG, early antigen), ASO, *Mycoplasma* IgG
- Tryptase if MCAS is suspected
- GI profile as indicated

## Treatment

### Goals [Clinical observation]

- Modulate immune function
- Optimize hypothalamic function
- Decrease inflammation
- Reset circadian rhythm

### Options

- Hypothalamic nutraceutical support
- Quercetin
- Curcumin
- Dopamine agonists [Rx overview]
- Low-dose naltrexone [Rx overview]
- Mushroom extracts
- Bone marrow glandulars
- Homeopathic viral support
- Sleep aids: melatonin, magnesium, valerian, GABA

### Hypothalamic support [Product] [Clinical observation]

In Deborah's experience, hypothalamic support:

- Supports the corticotroph, somatotroph, gonadotroph and thyrotroph axes, supporting healthy immune function
- Calms hypothalamic micro-inflammation
- Supports a healthy circadian rhythm and natural prolactin regulation

Products and doses:

- **Genesis Gold**, full hypothalamic support: 4 g per 50 lb body weight daily
- **Sacred Seven** hypothalamic amino acids: 5 g daily

### Quercetin [Emerging]

- **Use:** hypersensitivity, allergies, MCAS
- **Action:** mast cell stabilizer
- **Dose:** 250 to 500 mg daily
- **Pearls** [Clinical observation]: a loading dose of three times the daily dose at symptom onset for 3 to 7 days; then a maintenance dose for the rest of the season or until exposures are gone and mast cells are "retrained."

### Curcumin [Emerging]

- **Use:** inflammation
- **Action:** anti-inflammatory
- **Dose:** 500 mg daily; may need a loading dose of up to 1,500 mg daily
- **Pearls:** take with fat for absorption; piperine (BioPerine) increases bioavailability but also affects drug metabolism.

### Dopamine agonists [Rx overview]

- **Use:** high or dyscircadian prolactin
- **Action:** stimulate dopamine receptors, suppressing prolactin release
- **Deborah's approach** [Clinical observation]: bromocriptine 2.5 mg tablet at 8 am, given vaginally or rectally (off-label routes) to reduce the headache, blood pressure and other side effects seen with oral dosing.
- Titrate to bring the 8 am prolactin below 10 ng/mL; check prolactin monthly.
- Cabergoline is the guideline-preferred agent for prolactinomas. [Established]

### Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) [Rx overview] [Emerging]

- **Use:** MCAS
- **Action:** at low doses, naltrexone blocks Toll-like receptor 4 on microglia and other immune cells, reducing inflammatory signaling. Deborah also proposes that it restores communication between gut glial cells and the hypothalamus, reducing GI histamine. [Clinical observation]
- **Dose:** 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime
- **Pearls:** compounded; start low and increase by 1.5 mg weekly as needed; few side effects at low dose (vivid dreams are the most common). Avoid with opioid medications.

### Mushroom extracts [Emerging]

- **Use:** modulating immune response
- **Action:** act on hematopoietic stem cells, lymphocytes, macrophages, T cells, dendritic cells and NK cells
- **Dose:** 1,500 to 5,000 mg daily
- **Pearls:** divide doses and start low; 2 or 3 species may be combined.

### Bone marrow glandular [Clinical observation; limited published evidence]

- **Use:** low white blood cell count
- **Action:** intended to support white blood cell production
- **Dose:** 300 to 500 mg daily
- **Pearls:** recheck CBC with differential in 8 weeks. Investigate the cause of leukopenia first.

### Homeopathic viral support [Clinical observation; limited published evidence]

- **Use:** recurrent reactivated viruses
- **Intended action:** to prompt innate immunity against resident viruses
- **Dose:** 15 drops twice daily
- **Pearls:** formulas range from single to multiple viral species; use a specific formula when the virus is known.

### Sleep aids [Emerging]

- **Use:** insomnia affecting immune response
- **Action:** start sleep through GABA activity, or sustain sleep with melatonin

To start sleep:

- GABA liquid, 1 to 4 dropperfuls at bedtime
- Magnesium glycinate 200 mg at bedtime
- Valerian root 300 to 600 mg at bedtime

To sustain sleep:

- Slow-release melatonin 1.2 to 4.8 mg at bedtime
