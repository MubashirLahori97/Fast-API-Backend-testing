Nutrition Foundation — The DMAR® Healthy Four

How to calculate your lean body mass (LBM)

LBM = Total weight − (Total weight × Body fat %)

**Protein:** Eat 0.5–1 g × your LBM in pounds. Minimum 0.5 g for general maintenance; move toward 1 g when actively building muscle, recovering from injury, or losing weight to prevent muscle loss. Choose clean sources: pasture-raised eggs, wild fish, grass-fed meat, legumes.

**Fat:** 0.5 g × LBM. Prioritize omega-3s (wild fish, flaxseed, walnuts), omega-6s (vegetables, nuts, seeds), and omega-9s (extra-virgin olive oil). Eliminate trans fats entirely.

**Carbohydrates:** Keep carbs to 40% of total calories. If overweight or sedentary: 15 g per meal. At ideal weight with moderate activity: up to 50 g per meal. Athletic/highly active: up to 80 g per meal.

**Water:** Drink ½ oz per pound of body weight per day. Begin every morning with lemon water upon waking to stimulate digestive enzymes and kidney function.

Eat **½ cup of fresh beets** with dinner (not canned — the canning process interferes with the release of betalain, the natural red pigment that makes beets detectable in stool). Note the time. Watch for the distinctive pink or red color to appear in your stool. Note when it arrives.

**\< 8 hrs** Too Fast

Poor absorption; nutrients passing before uptake

**8–12 hrs** Optimal

Healthy transit, good absorption

**> 12 hrs** Too Slow

Constipation risk; toxin reabsorption

What your result means

**Under 8 hours:** Gut is moving too fast. Focus on fiber, healthy fats, and addressing underlying inflammation or stress. May need gut-lining support.

**8–12 hours:** Healthy transit. Keep doing what you're doing. You can include beets in your first liver cleanse dinner.

**Over 12 hours:** Slow transit — extend your liver cleanse to the full 7 days. Increase water (½ oz/lb body weight), add more fiber-rich vegetables, and rule out thyroid or hormonal causes with your provider.

HIIT Exercise Protocol

High-Intensity Interval Training 3 times per week reduces insulin resistance, builds lean mass, and improves cardiovascular function — in under 20 minutes per session.

Interval Structure

- 1 **Warm-up:** 5–7 minutes at comfortable, steady pace (walking, light cycling, easy rowing).
- 2 **High-intensity burst:** 20 seconds at maximum effort — run, cycle, row, or elliptical. You should not be able to hold a conversation.
- 3 **Recovery:** 60–90 seconds at slow, easy pace to bring heart rate down.
- 4 **Repeat:** complete 3 rounds of the 20-second burst + recovery cycle.
- 5 **Cool-down:** 5–7 minutes at comfortable pace. Stretch.

**Frequency:** 3 times per week. Rest at least one day between sessions. This is sufficient — more is not better with HIIT.

7 Secrets to Healthy Hormones

Seven small daily shifts that make a profound difference in hormonal health — from sleep to stress to connection. Each one speaks directly to your hypothalamus.

Secret #1

Sleep in the Dark

Your pineal gland can only produce melatonin in complete darkness. Any artificial light shuts off melatonin production — and without it, you never reach deep REM sleep. That keeps you in low-grade fight-or-flight, which disrupts every hormone downstream. Lack of adequate sleep is one of the leading causes of hypothalamic dysfunction, affecting sex hormones, weight, memory, and mood.

**Blackout curtains, no screens after dark, no nightlights.** Your hormones sleep in the dark too.

Secret #2

Eat Good Fats

Hormones are made from fat. Every steroid hormone — cortisol, estrogen, progesterone, testosterone, DHEA — contains a cholesterol molecule. Your cell membranes are built from fat, and if they're not healthy, hormone receptor sites can't function properly. Man-made fats can't be used by your body to build healthy cells.

**Choose:** natural saturated fats (butter, coconut oil), omega-3-rich fats (wild salmon, walnuts, flaxseed), and omega-9s (extra-virgin olive oil). These build the cell membranes that let your hormones work.

Secret #3

Limit Excess Carbohydrates

Glucose fuels energy production — but consuming more carbohydrates than you can expend leads to fat storage, insulin strain, and eventually insulin-resistant diabetes. Too much circulating sugar and insulin causes hypothalamic inflammation, especially combined with a high-fat diet.

**Cut:** processed carbs (flour, pasta, white rice). **Choose** whole fresh fruit over juice. Avoid sweetened beverages. Limit alcohol. Your hypothalamus will thank you.

Secret #4

Relax and Rejuvenate

Under stress, your adrenals enter fight-or-flight — and they don't distinguish between a missed appointment and a predator. It's the same response either way. All reserves fuel survival, leaving little for healthy hormone production. Chronic stress leads to hypothalamic dysfunction and adrenal fatigue.

**Every day:** make time to unwind. Take vacations. Move your body gently. Breathe. Your hypothalamus controls the stress response — give it relief and it will give your hormones harmony.

Secret #5

Give and Receive 12 Hugs a Day

Touch is therapeutic. Studies show that hugging measurably reduces stress hormones. Close physical contact also triggers the hypothalamus to produce **oxytocin** — the bonding hormone — and allows us to share pheromones, the tiny inhaled hormones that help us feel safe, choose a mate, and feel loved.

Connecting to others keeps your hormones healthy. Aim for at least 12 hugs a day.

Secret #6

Honor Your Natural Cycles

Your body responds to the seasons. At the end of winter, hormones rise — spring fever is real, and it's metabolic as well as reproductive. Thyroid, adrenal, pituitary, immune, and brain chemistry are all primed for the longer days ahead. Your body also responds to lunar cycles: emergency rooms are busier on the full moon, and women's cycles often sync with it.

Don't resist Mother Nature — listen to her. Go with the flow, and your hormones will harmonize.

Sleep — Deep Sleep & Longevity

Deep sleep is the only time your body runs its full repair program — glymphatic brain flush, growth hormone release, DNA repair, immune signaling. Short or fragmented deep sleep halts all of it. Research shows that sleeping outside the optimal range accelerates biological aging in nearly every organ.

The short version

- ◆ Deep sleep (slow-wave) = 10–20% of a normal night — about 40–110 minutes
- ◆ It clusters in the first third of the night — a consistent bedtime protects it better than sleeping in
- ◆ Your hypothalamus sets the timing for every repair process that follows
- ◆ Deep sleep declines with age — protecting what you have matters more each decade

Habit #1

Fix Your Bedtime — Same Time Every Night

Your hypothalamic circadian clock doesn't understand "catching up on sleep." It only understands consistency. Vary by no more than ±30 minutes. Stick with it for two weeks and you'll notice sleep onset accelerating — your brain has learned when to shut down.

Going to bed late costs you **deep sleep specifically** — not just sleep in general. Deep sleep front-loads into the first two cycles.

Habit #2

60-Minute Wind-Down Before Bed

For the 60 minutes before bed: no screens, no large meals, no intense exercise, no work messages.

- ◆ Blue light activates the hypothalamus, which suppresses melatonin (see Secret #1)
- ◆ A large meal raises core body temperature exactly when it needs to fall
- ◆ Work messages activate stress circuits that oppose sleep onset

These 60 minutes are your transition from high-alert mode to repair mode.

Habit #3

Keep Your Bedroom Cool: 64–68°F (18–20°C)

A drop in core body temperature is the physiological trigger for deep sleep onset. Room temperature isn't a comfort preference — it's a hormonal signal.

If you feel cold, **wear socks.** Warm feet dilate blood vessels in your extremities, which releases heat from your core and accelerates the temperature drop. Counterintuitive — but it works.

Habit #4

Stop Eating 3 Hours Before Bed

Late eating disrupts deep sleep three ways:

- ◆ **Core temperature:** digesting raises it just when it needs to drop to trigger slow-wave sleep
- ◆ **Reflux:** lying down on a full stomach fragments sleep even without fully waking
- ◆ **Clock mismatch:** feeding your liver, gut, and pancreas at 10 PM tells them it's still daytime while your brain is trying to shut down
